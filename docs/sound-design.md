# Vyrra’s interaction sounds

Studio latch is the default. The brighter opening and lower closing form a pair. A person can hear which direction the microphone changed while looking at the message field. Felt rounds off the attack for quieter use. Porcelain adds a short upper harmonic that carries on a small speaker. Classic and Soft click remain available, together with Off.

| Pair | Opening and closing length | Time containing 90% of energy, open / close | Spectral centroid, open / close |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Studio latch | 48 / 63 ms | 6.8 / 10.8 ms | 951 / 466 Hz |
| Felt thud | 55 / 77 ms | 10.2 / 14.8 ms | 462 / 307 Hz |
| Porcelain | 61 / 70 ms | 7.3 / 11.1 ms | 1,508 / 950 Hz |
| Classic | 52 / 65 ms | 9.3 / 14.2 ms | 943 / 655 Hz |
| Soft click | 46 / 61 ms | 11.1 / 14.5 ms | 555 / 337 Hz |

The three new pairs use Kenney’s CC0 Interface Sounds mixed with damped synthetic resonances. Studio uses two short contacts. The default opening true peak measures −12.8 dBFS, the close −18.9 dBFS. The comparison reel matches K-weighted energy over 100 ms per cue. These figures describe the audio files. Hardware, Bluetooth and OS scheduling affect when a person hears them. The recipe accepts the original pack and checks its digest. [Kenney Interface Sounds](https://kenney.nl/assets/interface-sounds)

Kaaresoja, Brewster and Lantz measured perceived touch feedback timing and reported a 20–70 ms range for audio feedback in their touchscreen experiments. That interval starts at touch and ends at sound onset. Apple's sound design examples show how related timbres can distinguish complementary actions and be learned over repeated use. An experimental study of physical push-button sounds found that frequency content affected judgments of confirmation and pleasantness. These findings guided the design. Preference and retention for Vyrra would require testing with actual listeners. [Touch-feedback timing study](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~stephen/papers/TAP%20-%20kaaresoja.pdf) · [Push-button sound study](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ast/41/1/41_E19205/_article) · [Apple’s Designing Sound session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2017/803/)

Gboard tells the user when it is ready for speech. Wispr documents output-route changes, including Bluetooth behavior, when using dictation. Vyrra follows the recorder’s readiness signal for its live wave and lets users turn the cues off. Desktop panel open and close sounds are separately selectable and off by default. Hover, tooltip reveal, live meter movement, routine processing and successful insertion are silent. A late error remains visible.
